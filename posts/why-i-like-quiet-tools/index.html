Why I Like Quiet Tools

Posted on Sep 30, 2026

Most software today is loud.

Whenever a new product launches, it wants to be everything at once. It comes with a massive dashboard, fifty settings menus, onboarding popups, and a constant stream of notifications.

Lately, AI tools have taken this to an extreme. To rewrite a sentence or fix a paragraph, we are often asked to switch tabs, open a dedicated workspace, copy-paste text back and forth, or deal with bloated sidebars taking over half the screen.

Every time we do that, we lose our train of thought.

Doing one thing well

I have always loved the Unix idea of small, focused tools. A good tool does not demand your attention. It waits quietly in the background until you need it, does its job in one second, and gets out of your way.

Think about a good physical tool, like a pen or a pocket knife. You do not think about the tool while using it. You just think about what you are making.

Software should feel the same way:

  1. Stay in your flow: You should never have to leave where you are already writing.
  2. No unnecessary friction: If an action takes more than one shortcut or click, it breaks your concentration.
  3. Respect your space: A tool should only appear when invoked and vanish immediately after.

Building for utility, not hype

Whenever I build small tools for myself—whether it is a quick browser extension to clean up writing or a simple script to automate a repetitive task—the goal is always subtraction.

What is the absolute minimum interface needed to solve this problem?

Usually, the answer is just a keyboard shortcut, a clear result, and a way to accept or reject it. No complex dashboards, no endless buttons.

Keep it simple

Technology is at its best when it feels invisible.

We do not need more apps trying to capture our attention for twenty minutes at a time. We need simple, reliable utilities that give us our time back so we can focus on our real work.
