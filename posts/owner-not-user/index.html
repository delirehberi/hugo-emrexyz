Owner, Not User

Posted on Sep 28, 2026 Tech

True digital ownership has always felt like a myth. For the longest time, you couldn’t actually own a piece of the internet. When you pay for web hosting, you’re just renting space on someone else’s server. When you sign up for Gmail, you don’t own that email address—you’re merely a user on an account ultimately controlled by Google. Across almost every platform on the web, you aren’t an owner; you are just a user.

In the physical world, ownership is simple: you buy a house, it’s yours. You buy a phone, you own it. So why did we collectively accept having zero ownership over our digital lives?

Historically, it came down to cost. Running your own email or web server used to be expensive and highly technical. Instead of owning, we chose to rent—and we paid our rent with our personal data (for free services). Over time, this rental model created massive centralized power. Today, tech giants use algorithms to shape what you see, what you like, and even what you believe. And legally, they have every right to do so, because we all clicked “Accept” on their terms and conditions.

Then came Bitcoin, which proved that true digital ownership through cryptography was not only possible but accessible. Inspired by this breakthrough, the Bitcoin community helped birth Nostr. For the first time, Nostr makes it possible to truly own your digital identity, completely independent of corporate gatekeepers.

We are witnessing a digital rebellion. The evolution of the internet is finally shifting power back in the right direction. Get ready, because the web as we know it is about to change.
